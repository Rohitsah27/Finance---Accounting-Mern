Complete 3-Tier Distribution Accounting Lifecycle

In modern commercial property & casualty insurance, premium cash can be billed, collected, and distributed across three standard industry operating models: Agency Bill (DBA), Direct Bill to MGA (DBM), and Direct Bill to Carrier (DBC). Below is the complete step-by-step lifecycle for each flow, including exact double-entry ledger postings across all participants.

Model 1
DBA (Agency Bill)

Insured → Broker ($10k) → MGA ($9k) → Carrier ($8.5k)
Retail agency collects cash from client, deducts commission, and remits net upward.

Model 2
DBM (Direct Bill to MGA)

Insured → MGA ($10k) → Carrier ($8.5k) + Broker ($1k)
MGA program manager collects cash, pays producer commission, and settles with carrier.

Model 3
DBC (Direct Bill to Carrier)

Insured → Carrier ($10k) → MGA ($1.5k) → Broker ($1k)
Carrier collects full premium directly from insured and disburses commission downward.

Model 1 Agency Bill (DBA)

1. DBA (Direct Bill to Agency / Agency Bill)

Run in Live Simulator

In the Agency Bill (DBA) model, the retail insurance broker/agency issues the billing statement directly to the policyholder (Insured). The customer remits the gross premium of $10,000 to the Broker's Premium Trust account. The Broker deducts their 10% commission ($1,000) and remits $9,000 net to the MGA. The MGA takes a 5% override ($500) and remits $8,500 net-net to the Carrier.

Flow Sequence: Insured → Broker ($10,000) → MGA ($9,000) → Carrier ($8,500)

Model 2 Direct Bill to MGA (DBM)

2. DBM (Direct Bill to MGA)

Run in Live Simulator

In the Direct Bill to MGA (DBM) model, the Managing General Agent (MGA) acts as the central fiduciary hub. The MGA invoices the customer directly for $10,000. When cash is received into the MGA trust, the MGA remits $1,000 commission outward to the Broker, wires $8,500 net to the Carrier, and retains $500 as net override revenue.

Flow Sequence: Insured → MGA ($10,000) → Carrier ($8,500) + Broker ($1,000)

Model 3 Direct Bill to Carrier (DBC)

3. DBC (Direct Bill to Carrier)

Run in Live Simulator

In the Direct Bill to Carrier (DBC) model, the insurance carrier directly bills and collects the gross $10,000 premium from the customer. Once cash is secured in the Carrier's bank, the Carrier remits a combined commission wire of $1,500 ($1,000 Broker commission + $500 MGA override) to the MGA. The MGA deposits the $1,500, retains $500 as override revenue, and disburses $1,000 to the Broker.

Flow Sequence: Insured → Carrier ($10,000) → MGA ($1,500) → Broker ($1,000)
